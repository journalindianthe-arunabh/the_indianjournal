The JP movement in 1973-74, which began as a student-led agitation against corruption, unemployment and poor governance, which led to the declaration of emergency and later to the fall of the Indira Government in 1977. More than fifty year later in 2026 we saw another major youth led protest at Jantar Mantar led by the Abhijeet Dipke. It all started when our honourable Chief Justice of India (CJI), Justice Surya Kant was hearing a plea involving individuals who allegedly entered professional fields, including the legal system, using fake or fraudulent degrees. His oral remark took to the social media by flood which led to the formation of the Cockroach Janta party (CJP). After which the events unfolded faster then expected and later led to the resignation of the Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan on 25th July 2026. More than 600 NTA officials have also been removed and the appointment for the same is under the process since then. The CJP protest was not only seen at Jantar Mantar, New Delhi its effect has been seen around the country from Lucknow, Patna, Mumbai to Hyderabad, Jammu, Bhuvaneswar. People come on the roads for the change in the education system and the problems in the examination. It was first of a kind protest which was not publicly backed by any of the political party, major opposition parties like Congress and Aam Aadmi Party came in the support towards the end of the protest.

The success of the CJP protest led the youth of the country to talk about the various issues which has been the topic of debate in the country since decades now. One such issue is the issue of the reservation in this country. The formation of the Reservation Hatao Andolan (RHA) page on Instagram and the start of the protest at some parts of the country for the same, shows the power of the social media. But the question is can the reservation be removed and should it be removed?

The idea of reservation in India first evolved in 1882 when Jyotirao Phule and William Hunter suggested the caste based reservation in the Education commission. Later reservation was extended to various caste and class. In 1932 Poona Pact was signed between Mahatma Gandhi and Dr. B.R. Ambedkar abandoning separate electorates for Dalits in Favor of reserved seats within a joint Hindu electorate. The issue of reservation was taken by the constituent assembly and in 1950, in which temporary reservation was provided for Scheduled Castes (SCs) and Scheduled Tribes (STs) in legislatures and public employment. The temporary reservation was for the period of ten years initially, which kept on increasing each time by constitutional amendment. Reservation in India was never just a poverty-relief programme. Its primary purpose was to address the historic inequality in opportunity, suppression and discrimination by the upper class.

Articles 15(4) and 15(5) enables the State to make special provisions for socially and educationally backward classes and for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes in education, while Article 16(4) provides for reservation in public employment for backward classes that, in the State's opinion, are not adequately represented in public services. Therefore, reservation is not an ordinary government welfare scheme that can simply be discontinued by an executive decision. It is embedded within the constitutional framework of equality and affirmative action.

In the landmark Indra Sawhney case, the Supreme Court accepted caste as one determinant of backwardness while also emphasising the need to exclude the socially and educationally advanced sections through the principle of the creamy layer. The Court also developed the well-known 50 per cent ceiling principle in the context of reservations under Article 16(4), while recognising that exceptional circumstances could require consideration.

There are various legitimate questions about whether the benefits of reservation are reaching the people who need them most. The concentration of benefits among relatively advanced sections within some reserved categories, inadequate identification of the most disadvantaged groups, differences between states and the absence of verification of the people getting the benefits of the reservation is causing the major issue in the reservation system.

The debate over reservation can never end in a country like India which has different caste and class biases. There are genuine concerns about its implementation, unequal distribution of benefits, the creamy layer and whether the most disadvantaged sections are receiving adequate opportunities. These concerns require periodic review, transparency and reform. Youth have every right to question the system, but the debate should be based on evidence rather than slogans.
